What is Digital Harassment?

Digital harassment refers to bullying, threats, stalking, or abuse carried out through online platforms such as social media, emails, messaging apps, and gaming communities.

Helping others is important in our lives.

Common Types of Digital Harassment

Digital abuse takes many forms. Understanding these behaviors is the first step toward recognizing and stopping them.

Cyberbullying & Trolling
Sending, posting, or sharing malicious, false, or mean content about someone to deliberately humiliate them or bait them into arguments.
Cyberstalking
The persistent, unwanted monitoring and tracking of a person's digital footprint, online status, or physical location to create a state of fear.
Doxxing
Researching and publicly broadcasting private or identifying information (like home addresses or phone numbers) without consent to invite real-world harm.
Impersonation
Creating fake profiles or hijacking existing accounts to pose as the victim, often with the goal of damaging their friendships or reputation.
Hate Speech & Brigading
Coordinated group attacks targeting individuals or communities based on protected characteristics like race, religion, gender, or disability.
Non-Consensual Image Sharing
Distributing intimate, sensitive, or private photos and videos of someone without their explicit permission to blackmail or humiliate them.

How to Prevent Digital Harassment

  • Protect Your Privacy

    Use strong, unique passwords and strictly limit the amount of personal information you share online.

  • Block and Report

    Do not engage with trolls. Block abusive accounts and report harmful behavior to platform administrators immediately.

  • Think Before Sharing

    Avoid posting sensitive assets like physical addresses, school schedules, photos, or deeply personal data.

  • Stay Respectful

    Be the change you want to see. Promote digital empathy, kindness, and positive communication across digital platforms.

Need Help?

If you or someone you know is currently experiencing online harassment, you do not have to handle it alone. Talk to a trusted adult, teacher, counselor, or contact dedicated online safety organizations for support.
